# TheLife 🌱

A deep, text-based life simulator for Android in the spirit of InstaLife/BitLife. Live one year at a time from birth to death, make choices, and build a family legacy across generations.

## Features

- **17 countries**, each with its own names, currency, tax rate and life expectancy. Emigrate mid-life.
- **Born talents**: about half of people are born with a gift (a musical ear, a natural actor, a born athlete, a gifted mind, striking looks, an artistic eye, a silver tongue or an iron constitution), a few with two or three. Gifts work from birth, raising a career's ceiling or making a skill come easier, but you discover them as a kid when a teacher or coach notices. About me lists them, and heirs can inherit a parent's gift.
- **Stats**: happiness, health, smarts, looks, plus athleticism, karma, fame and social-media followers. Smarts grow fastest early and slow near the top, thinking jobs keep you sharp, and old age dulls them; debt hurts your mood in proportion to how much you owe. Karma is a reputation: it fades slowly back toward neutral, so it reflects how you've been living lately, and the life log tells you when people start to see you as good, kind, shady or cruel. Happiness settles toward a baseline set by your life (money, love, a job or school, friends, pets, health) and by your habits: exercising, meditating, reading and practicing hobbies each year lift it. About me shows your mood baseline and what's moving it. Small repeated gains (a second gym trip) still add up instead of rounding away. Everyday activities (gym, library, meditation, walks, martial arts, diets, instruments) give solid gains that tire out gently when repeated in a year, and results show exactly what moved, like "(+4 smarts, +2 health)" (fun activities like fishing, spa days, nightclubs, skydiving and marathons too). Hobby skills grow faster (up to six practice sessions a year that tire out gently), each session says how much you gained, and hobbies feed the rest of life: chess, coding and writing sharpen your mind, art, singing and comedy lift your mood, cooking keeps you healthy. Relatives whose health hits zero don't linger. Vacations differ by destination: adventure trips build athleticism and health, culture trips (museums and history) add smarts, luxury escapes restore health and looks, and Carnival is pure fun at a cost to your body. Pricier trips go deeper, and a vacation counts as self-care for your mood baseline. A passing cold costs your mood baseline less than a chronic illness.
- **Education**: elementary → middle → high school, a GED, trade school, university (26 majors; pay with parents, a scholarship, loans or savings), and graduate school (Law, Medicine, Veterinary, Dental, MBA, PhD). You can study, join clubs and sports teams, skip class or drop out.
- **Careers**: 50 career ladders with promotions, performance reviews, raises and firing, plus part-time jobs, odd jobs, retirement and pensions. **Job interviews** ask you a question, and your answer (or your smarts, fitness or looks) sways the hiring manager. Working hard pays off: performance no longer drifts down on its own, and a promotion only partly resets it, so a hard worker climbs about twice as fast as someone coasting.
- **Office life**: every regular job has a boss with a personality (supportive, demanding, a micromanager, hands-off, political or a visionary) and a relationship that sways raises and promotions. Lead big projects (or play it safe), have one-on-ones (ask for feedback, pitch an idea, or flatter), choose how big a raise to ask for, and look for outside offers to take or use as leverage. A yearly performance review gives a rating and a merit raise, and your job card shows exactly what you need for the next promotion.
- **Trade school, vet school and dental school**: become an electrician, plumber, welder or HVAC tech after two years of training, or a veterinarian or dentist after grad school.
- **Astronaut**: a tough-to-land career for fit STEM graduates, with real space missions (and real risk).
- **Military career**: talk to a recruiter, take the ASVAB, and enlist in a real branch (U.S. Army, Marines, Navy, Air Force, Coast Guard or Space Force, or your country's forces) in a job like Infantry, Combat Medic, Military Police or Cyber Operations, on a 4- or 6-year contract with a signing bonus. Survive basic training, then rise through real ranks (Private to Sergeant Major, Second Lieutenant to General, or the Navy's ranks) with realistic pay, housing allowance and promotion boards. Deploy overseas, earn real medals (Bronze Star, Purple Heart, Silver Star, Medal of Honor) or come home with PTSD, try out for the Rangers, Green Berets, Navy SEALs, Marine Raiders or Pararescue, and re-enlist, leave as a veteran, or retire after 20 years with a pension. Officers come from OCS, ROTC or West Point, Annapolis and the Air Force Academy. Veterans get the GI Bill and hiring preference; crimes mean a court-martial, and South Korean men face conscription. As a soldier you build four skills (fitness, marksmanship, leadership and tactics) through PT, range qualification, study and mentoring, and they decide your evaluations, promotions and how deployments go. Deployments can turn into a defining mission (an ambush, a booby-trapped cache, a sniper, a warning about a night attack) where you choose how to respond. NCOs and officers hold real billets (squad leader, platoon sergeant, company commander…) and lead a unit whose cohesion matters. Attend Airborne, Ranger and Sniper School or serve as a drill instructor to earn badges, get new orders every few years (a stateside base, an overseas posting, or a front-line unit), and keep it all in a service record of medals, schools, deployments and duty stations.
- **Basketball career**: make your high school varsity team (5-star recruits become McDonald's All-Americans), earn college scholarship offers from real programs (Duke, Kentucky, UConn…), play March Madness (Round of 64 to the national title) and chase All-American honors, then declare for the NBA Draft (or the WNBA for women). Your player has four attributes (shooting, playmaking, defense, athleticism) shaped by your position and by what you train, which decide your archetype (3-and-D wing, shot creator, rim protector…), your badges (Sharpshooter, Floor General, Lockdown Defender, Highlight Dunker) and your stat line (points, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, FG% and 3P%). Train to raise your rating toward your potential; training near your ceiling pushes it higher once a year until 31, and getting more athletic as a teen raises it too. Teams are contenders or rebuilding, and it carries over year to year, so free agency is a choice between money and a ring. Every season has a record, a season-high game (50-point nights included) and a playoff run, and some runs come down to Game 7 with the ball in your hands: pull up for three, attack the rim or find the open man. Win championships, MVP, Finals MVP, All-NBA teams, Defensive Player of the Year, Sixth Man, Most Improved, scoring titles, Rookie of the Year and All-Star selections, enter the Three-Point and Slam Dunk Contests, and play in the Olympics every four years. A trophy case keeps every award with its years; tap any season for the full stat line. Sign contracts, request trades, land a signature shoe deal, survive injuries, get called up from the G League or play in the EuroLeague, retire your jersey and make the Hall of Fame. The league matches your era: real NBA and WNBA franchises with the names they had that year (Seattle SuperSonics until 2008, New Jersey Nets until 2012, no WNBA before 1997), the CBA, D-League or G League as the minors, salaries scaled to the era, and real stars as your teammates and opponents (Jordan, Kobe, LeBron, Curry, Taurasi, Caitlin Clark…). History before your rookie season is real; from your first pro season (or from 2026, where real data ends) the whole league is simulated: every team has a rated roster seeded from that season's real stars (rated by how great they really became), players develop, peak, decline and retire, a lottery draft brings in new prospects, free agents re-sign or chase contenders, stars on losing teams get traded, and the league sometimes expands. Records come from team strength (you included), playoff series are played game by game, and MVP, Rookie of the Year and Finals MVP go to whoever earns them. When your series reaches a deciding game, the bracket waits for your last shot. The League screen has standings by conference, every champion and MVP (real history, then simulated), the top players, the year's moves and retirements, and team pages with full rosters. Basketball social media follows the era too (MySpace, Facebook, Twitter, Instagram, TikTok): build a following, post highlight reels and vlogs, go live, call out rivals, take sponsored posts, get verified, read what fans and the media say in your feed, get pulled into beefs, and sign NIL deals in college from 2022. Athletic players have real star ceilings, and steady training keeps raising yours. Each pro season you pick a game plan: score-first (more points and All-Star votes, a bit less winning), team-first (more assists, your team wins more) or lockdown defense (steals, blocks and a shot at Defensive Player of the Year). Break out on a rookie deal and your team offers an extension (a max deal if you're a superstar): sign it, bet on yourself in free agency, or demand more and risk a trade or a fan revolt.
- **School sports**: try out for basketball, soccer, football, baseball, track, swimming, volleyball, wrestling, tennis or cheer from middle school on. Practice, play the big rivalry game, and play a real season every year with stats for your sport (goals, touchdowns, batting average, race times…). Win state titles, make All-State, become team captain, earn an athletic scholarship to a real university, and get drafted by a real pro team. Your team carries over from middle school to high school. Your skill grows toward a ceiling that rises with your athleticism, and practicing hard near your ceiling pushes it higher, so dedicated athletes really can get recruited and drafted.
- **Content creator**: start a YouTube, TikTok or Twitch channel in one of nine niches, film videos, go viral, collab, upgrade your gear, land sponsors, launch merch, earn Play Buttons (100K, 1M, 10M) and go full-time. Watch out for burnout, creator drama and copyright strikes.
- **Side hustles**: drive for Uber, deliver for DoorDash, freelance on Fiverr, flip sneakers on StockX, walk dogs on Rover, tutor, host on Airbnb or hold a garage sale.
- **Crypto**: buy Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, Dogecoin and short-lived meme coins through four-year boom and bust cycles (rug pulls and exchange hacks included). Prices swing hard but around a slowly rising fair value, so a coin can 10x in a boom without drifting to absurd prices over a lifetime.
- **Social media**: real platforms by era (MySpace until it dies, Facebook, Twitter that becomes X in 2023, Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok), each with its own followers and audience. Pick a niche, post what suits each platform (selfies, memes, threads, hot takes, dance videos, skits, vlogs, thirst traps, songs, snap stories, lives with tips), and join the year's real viral trend (the Ice Bucket Challenge, the Mannequin Challenge, brat summer…). Followers grow when you stay active and fade when you don't; engagement falls as you get bigger and when you buy bots. Brand deals match your niche: haggle, sign multi-year ambassador contracts, or take money from sketchy sponsors that can blow up later. Your DMs bring collabs, scams, fan mail, haters, celebrity follows and people who want to date you; old posts resurface and get you canceled (apologize, Notes-app apology, double down or go quiet). Influencer tiers, verification (or a paid blue check on X), a feed of your posts with top comments, analytics with your follower history, doomscrolling and social media breaks. Social media is tied to the world: a Trending now list each year pulls in real news and history (COVID, the 2008 crash, the iPhone), the year's market headline, the box-office hit and Best Picture, NBA and WNBA champions, elections, the Olympics and the World Cup, and you choose an honest take, a joke (or a respectful tribute) or a spicy hot take. Share your own big moments (graduation, wedding, new baby, new home, new job, promotion, a big win) and family and friends react; promote your actual movie, music, business, campaign or team for real effects; and scroll a timeline of what the people in your life are posting, where likes, kind comments and snarky ones change your relationships. Fame pulls in followers whether you post or not: movie stars, musicians, pro athletes and politicians gain followers in line with their careers (smaller in the early platform years), the log says where they came from, and a famous person with no accounts gets fans asking where to follow them.
- **People with minds of their own**: the people in your life remember what you did to them (gifts, insults, fights, favors, loans) and hold grudges or forgive depending on their personality. Each person's card shows their mood, what they think of you and why, what they remember, and any money they owe you or you've invested with them. Every year a few of them act on their own: invitations, gifts, looking after you when you're sick, prison visits, bragging about you, gossip and jealousy, loan requests (paid back or not), favors, secrets they confide, confrontations over old grudges, business pitches that boom or bust, and jealous partners. They have lives of their own too: promotions, layoffs, new jobs, weddings, babies, moves, health scares and retirement. Conversations talk back: ask how someone is really doing and they answer in their own words (their promotion, their layoff, the new baby, the grudge they still hold), then comfort them, give advice or change the subject; ask for their advice, gossip (some people love it, kind ones won't), clear the air about an old fight, or tell them what they mean to you. Their card shows how they greet you. When you go viral or get canceled, loyal friends cheer you on or defend you and jealous ones pile on, and they remember it.
- **Phone**: from 1996 a button in the header opens your computer (a phone from 2007) with Buzzline, a social site full of reviews and hot takes. The Movies tab has the year's real blockbuster and the latest real Best Picture winner, critic scores (on Rotten Tomatoes from 1999) and star ratings for new releases, and reviews of your own films. The Sports tab has last season's real NBA and WNBA champions and MVPs, hot takes on real stars, and fan ratings of your own seasons. Music, TV and About me tabs cover your albums, your show and what people say about you. Post your own reviews (some go viral) or jump to your social media account.
- **Acting**: start as a child actor or later in life. Pick from real casting calls (commercials, student films, theater, TV guest spots, indie films on A24 or Neon, TV series on HBO or Netflix, Broadway, studio films and blockbusters), choose how to prepare for each audition, train at The Groundlings or the Lee Strasberg Institute, get headshots, sign with an agency (up to CAA or WME), network at industry parties and do press tours. Every credit builds your star power (good reviews and leading roles faster), which opens bigger casting calls. Each project releases with a Rotten Tomatoes score and box office, TV shows get renewed or canceled, and great work earns Golden Globe, Emmy, Tony and Academy Award nominations. Parts that fit you (comedy chops, athleticism for action, a singing voice for musicals, looks for romance) are easier to land and review better. Series regulars can ask for a raise (it might get your character killed off), and stars can produce their own indie or studio film and keep a share of the box office. Franchise offers, passion projects and tabloid scandals along the way; your filmography keeps every credit. Your range has four skills (drama, comedy, action and screen presence) shaped by the classes you take (including stunt and fight training) and the parts you play; play the same kind of part too often and you get typecast, until a great against-type lead breaks you out. Every project has a director: work with one again and the reviews get better, and a director who loves your work makes you their muse and sends you more scripts. Go method for big dramatic leads (it costs your health, but critics notice), earn a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame and, late in a great career, an Honorary Oscar. A career highlights screen collects your awards, box office, best reviews and favorite director. Films have production budgets, opening weekends and box office in the money of the day, and a studio wants about 2.5 times its budget back, so every release is a bomb, a flop, a break-even, a hit or a smash. Hits can become franchises: sequels every few years (with numbered or subtitled titles), offers you can sign, hold out on or walk away from (the studio recasts you), multi-picture contracts you can only break by paying up, franchise fatigue, spin-offs for scene-stealing supporting parts, and a legacy sequel decades later. A Franchises section tracks every film in each series. Studios, networks and agencies match the era (no Marvel Studios before 2008, Fox Searchlight before Searchlight, William Morris before WME, network TV before streaming), and reviews are counted on Rotten Tomatoes only from 1999. Hollywood follows the era: real directors (Hitchcock, Spielberg, Scorsese, Nolan, Gerwig…) direct your films and real stars of the day co-star (Bogart and Bergman, Hanks and Roberts, Zendaya and Chalamet…), with invented names filling the future. Silent pictures before 1928, radio ads before TV, no TV shows before 1949, seven-year studio contracts in the 1930s–50s (a steady check, but the studio picks your pictures), streaming movies from 2015 that report households instead of box office, near-empty theaters in 2020, and the 2023 SAG-AFTRA strike. Reviews come from the critics of the day (the New York Times, Siskel & Ebert's thumbs, Roger Ebert's stars), and you can charm (or bomb on) the talk shows of the era, from Johnny Carson and Oprah to Fallon and Colbert. Leads in big studio pictures negotiate (from 1950, after Jimmy Stewart's famous deal): take the full fee, or half plus back-end points that pay a share of the box office. Every part has its own preparation: do your own stunts (and maybe get hurt), train with a vocal coach or get dubbed in a musical, master an accent, improvise in a comedy, stay in character for horror, do chemistry reads for a romance, or go method in a drama. Things happen on set: clashes with the director, reshoots, a co-star you learn from or a feud that sells tickets, accidents. Oscar and Golden Globe nominations come with an awards-season campaign you can spend big on, keep modest, or skip. Voice roles in animated films arrive with the studios of their day (Disney from 1937, Pixar from 1995, DreamWorks Animation, Illumination). Early credits move your reputation the most, and working actors only fade slowly (a year with nothing on screen hurts), so a steady actor becomes a working actor in a few years and a dedicated one can be a star by about 30.
- **Music**: pick a genre (pop, hip hop, R&B, rock, country, EDM, indie, Latin or jazz) and a stage name, take voice lessons or a Berklee summer program, and write songs whose quality depends on your musicianship. Release singles (with a music video or a TikTok tease) and EPs or albums (record at home, in a studio or with a star producer); every release wins new fans (great songs reach far more new listeners) and gets streams, Billboard Hot 100 or Billboard 200 chart positions, Metacritic scores and Gold, Platinum or Diamond certifications. Play open mics, bars and club, theater, arena and stadium tours. Stay independent or sign with a real label (Sub Pop, XL, Big Machine, Republic, Atlantic, Columbia, Interscope, Def Jam) for an advance, a royalty rate and albums owed. Do features, play Coachella or Glastonbury, survive beefs and leaks, and get nominated for Grammys. Your discography keeps everything. Your artist has four crafts (songwriting, vocals or flow, production and performance) shaped by your genre and by what you work on, which give you an archetype (hitmaker, storyteller, studio auteur…). Every song you write has a hook and a depth: chase a catchy hook, write something personal, or pay for a co-write with a hit songwriter. Catchy songs stream as singles; deep songs make albums critics love. Price your tours (cheap tickets for new fans, premium for money), sell merch, open for a superstar's arena tour, license songs to Netflix shows, ads and video games, win MTV VMAs for your music videos, track weeks at No. 1, and get into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame 25 years after your first release. A career highlights screen collects your hits, awards and milestones. Your potential shows as stars on your music card, and hits build buzz that carries your next releases (and fades if you go quiet). Pick which song to release as a single, post songs online the way each era did it (demo tapes to radio DJs, MP3s, MySpace, YouTube, SoundCloud, TikTok) for a shot at a breakout, win (or bomb) the school talent show, and get discovered by a producer. New artists grow their fanbase faster. Writing, releasing and staying active all make you a better musician. Once you have a following, your features are with real artists of the era in your genre (Dolly Parton and Garth Brooks for country, Dr. Dre and Kendrick Lamar for hip hop, Bad Bunny and Karol G for Latin...), real headliners invite you to open their tours, and big stars call asking you onto their next single. Your collaborators are listed in the studio. Every album or EP gets a rollout: a lead single first (steady), a midnight surprise drop (huge for big stars, a flop for small ones), a paid promo blitz with radio, interviews and a real late-night TV performance, or a visual album (a video for every song, loved by critics). Once you're big, a real star features on the record.
- **Pro sports**: football, soccer, baseball, hockey and tennis careers (basketball has its own mode). Get drafted out of college or win an open tryout (your school skill, athleticism and age set the odds), start in the minors (practice squad, Triple-A, AHL, USL, Challenger Tour) or on a real team (NFL, MLS, Premier League, La Liga, MLB, NPB, NHL, ATP…; NWSL, WSL, PWHL and WTA for women). Your overall rating grows toward your potential and declines with age; train (hard training can raise your potential while you're young), rehab, give press conferences, request trades and start a foundation. Every season has a real stat line for your position, a team record, playoffs and championships (Super Bowl, World Series, Stanley Cup, Grand Slams), Pro Bowl / All-Star nods, Rookie of the Year and MVPs (Ballon d'Or, Cy Young, Hart Trophy). Injuries, call-ups, trades, releases, free agency offers that weigh your age, endorsement deals, retirement, jersey retirements and the Hall of Fame. Your player has four attributes named for the sport (pace, physical, technique and vision in soccer; velocity, command and pitch IQ for pitchers; skating and hands in hockey…) shaped by your position and by what you train, including new speed and agility drills; they decide your archetype and color your stat lines. Teams are contenders or rebuilding and stay that way for a while, so free agency weighs money against winning. Some playoff runs come down to one moment (fourth and goal, a shootout penalty, bottom of the ninth, Game 7 overtime) and you choose how to play it. Win sport-specific awards (All-Pro, Golden Boot, Golden Glove, Silver Slugger, Gold Glove, Norris, Vezina, Art Ross, Comeback Player of the Year), have signature games (hat tricks, walk-off grand slams, no-hitters, 50-save shutouts), play in the World Cup or the Winter Olympics, and keep it all in a trophy case. Strong athletes and born athletes have real star ceilings, and steady training keeps raising yours. Each season you pick an approach: go all out (play above your rating, risk more injuries), stay healthy (fewer injuries, a few less minutes) or lead the locker room (a better team and a shot at being voted captain), which goes in your trophy case). Outplay your contract and your team offers an extension: take the security, bet on yourself and test free agency (worth about 12% more if you stay healthy), or, if you're a star, hold out for a bigger deal and risk an ugly standoff.
- **Politics**: run for City Council, then climb to Mayor, Governor, Senator and President. You campaign, give speeches, pass policies, survive scandals, take (or refuse) bribes, win re-elections and hit term limits. Pick a party (Democrat, Republican or Independent in the US; Labour, Conservative and others elsewhere) and build four political skills (rhetoric, fundraising, policy and grassroots). Hold fundraisers to fill a campaign war chest that pays for campaigns, face a live debate before each election (attack, win on details or stay positive), and propose bills from your party's agenda to build a legislative record. Every office has its own power: fight for your district, launch city projects, sign or veto bills as governor, filibuster as a senator, sign executive orders as president. Handle crises, accept endorsements, and after office write a memoir and give paid speeches; presidents get a presidential library and a ranking from historians. Reaching the presidency now takes real effort: national races are harder and parties tire of repeat losers.
- **Relationships**: pick what to do together (dinner, a hike, video games, a museum…) and what to talk about; people's traits decide whether they love it or are bored. Parents, siblings, grandparents, friends, classmates, coworkers, partners and kids. You can talk, gift, prank, argue, and ask for or give money. Dating app, nightclubs, vacation romances, proposals, weddings (with prenups), cheating, divorce with asset splits, pregnancy, twins, IVF, surrogacy and adoption. Your adult children marry and give you grandchildren. When things get rocky with a partner, try couples counseling: a kind, loyal partner usually meets you halfway and forgives old hurts, a stubborn or jealous one rarely budges, and a bad session in a failing marriage can end it.
- **Celebrity dating**: once you're famous, rich or working in entertainment or sports, an invite-only app lets you date stars who fit the era: movie stars, pop stars and rappers, NBA, WNBA, NFL and soccer stars, tennis champions, supermodels, reality stars, influencers and tech billionaires (all invented people). Your fame and looks against theirs decide it, working in the same industry helps, and a party introduction improves your odds. Dating a star brings fame, paparazzi, red carpets and tabloid rumors, and a famous single might slide into your DMs first. Real stars of the era cross your path too (actors, singers, NBA and WNBA players, sports legends): ask for a selfie, an autograph or a chat, and once you're famous they may already be fans of your work. Every encounter goes into your Stars I've met collection.
- **Where you live**: stay with your parents (they may start charging rent), rent anything from a shared room to a luxury loft, move into a house you own, or end up evicted and homeless.
- **Assets**: real estate with mortgages, renovation and rental income; cars, jets and yachts with financing; jewelry; and a stock market.
- **Stock market**: real companies (Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia, Tesla, Amazon, Walmart, Coca-Cola, Pfizer, Goldman Sachs) plus an S&P 500 index fund and a bond fund. Every year the whole market has a good or bad year, with the occasional crash or boom, and each stock follows it more or less closely, plus its own company news (earnings beats, lawsuits, product launches). Stocks pay dividends and split when prices climb. Each stock has its own page with a 10-year price chart, risk, dividend, your average cost and gain or loss; the portfolio card tracks total value, all-time gain, dividends and realized gains. Auto-invest a share of every paycheck into the index fund. Crashes let you panic sell, hold or buy the dip. The market follows the S&P 500's real return for every year from 1928 to 2025 (Black Tuesday, Black Monday, the dot-com bust, 2008, COVID, the AI boom), with the year's market headline in your log, and the famous real stock years happen too: Apple near bankruptcy in 1997 then the iMac, iPod and iPhone; Amazon's dot-com run and 80% crash; Tesla's 2020 moonshot; Nvidia's AI boom; Pfizer's vaccine year. After 2025 the market is random.
- **Ancient Rome**: choose "Ancient Rome" when you're born and live a whole life in the Roman Empire (AD 40–160), paid in denarii, with Roman names and cities from Rome and Pompeii to Londinium and Alexandria. Your social class shapes everything: patricians can study rhetoric and climb the cursus honorum from quaestor to consul (and govern a province, honestly or not), plebeians work as farmers, bakers, smiths, merchants, scribes, teachers, physicians, advocates or priests, and people born into slavery can save their peculium to buy their freedom, be freed by their master, or risk running away. Join the legions and march to real campaigns (Britannia, Dacia, Parthia) from legionary to centurion, fight as a gladiator for the wooden sword of freedom, or race chariots for the Greens or the Blues. Or become a philosopher: pick the Stoics, Epicureans, Cynics or Platonists, write treatises ("On the Shortness of Life"), debate in the Forum, teach pupils and rise to head of a school; dodge Domitian's banishment of philosophers, maybe tutor the emperor's heir (careful under Nero), and if you're enslaved, listen to philosophers in the street like Epictetus did. Girls from citizen families can be chosen as Vestal Virgins between 6 and 10: thirty years keeping Vesta's sacred fire (ten learning, ten serving, ten teaching), pardoning condemned prisoners, guarding the wills of the powerful and maybe rising to Vestalis Maxima. Break the vow of chastity and you may be buried alive. Go to the baths, the games, the races and the theater, throw banquets, make offerings to the gods, consult an augur, buy a domus, a villa or farmland, finance trade voyages and lend money. Emperors come and go, and history happens: the Great Fire, the Year of the Four Emperors, the Colosseum's opening, Vesuvius (if you live near Pompeii, flee!), Hadrian's Wall and the Antonine Plague. Arranged marriages, Saturnalia, insula fires, Tiber floods, tax collectors and patrons fill the years.
- **Medieval England**: choose "Medieval England" when you're born and live in England between 1150 and 1420, paid in silver pennies, with English names in London, York, Norwich or a village like Ashby. Birth decides almost everything. Serfs (villeins) work their lord's land and can buy a charter of freedom or flee to a town, where a year and a day makes them free. Free peasants and townsfolk can learn a trade in the guilds (smith, baker, weaver, carpenter, mason, brewer, tailor, barber-surgeon), going from apprentice to journeyman, then submitting a masterpiece to become a master and maybe warden of the guild. Gentry and noble boys go to a great house as pages, become squires, are knighted at 21, joust in tournaments for the loser's horse and armor, and go to war in Scotland and France, ransoming captured lords or being ransomed themselves. Join the king's archers (be at Agincourt), become a monk or nun and rise to abbot or abbess, become a priest and rise to bishop (that needs Oxford or Cambridge), or seek a place at court, where favor can make you Lord Chancellor and suspicion can land you in the Tower. Women can become midwives (rising, with fame, to midwife to the queen), and anyone devout enough can be walled into a cell beside a church for life as an anchoress or anchorite, counseling those who come to the window and writing down visions, like Julian of Norwich. Learn letters from the parish priest and you can plead benefit of clergy (the "neck verse") if you're ever tried for your life. Go to the alehouse, the fair, mystery plays and tournaments, go on pilgrimage to Canterbury, Walsingham, Santiago, Rome or Jerusalem, buy indulgences, poach the king's deer, buy a cottage, a manor house with rent-paying tenants or a castle, ship wool to Flanders and lend money despite the Church. Kings come and go, and history happens: Becket's murder, the crusades and Richard's ransom, Magna Carta, the Great Famine, the Black Death (flee, nurse the sick, or bar the door and pray) and its later returns, the Peasants' Revolt, Chaucer, Joan of Arc, the Wars of the Roses (pick York or Lancaster and live with it), Bosworth, the printing press and, for long lives, Henry VIII closing the monasteries. Conditions get their medieval names, like the megrim, a quinsy and dotage. When you continue as your heir in Rome or medieval England, the family's standing carries on: a patrician's or noble's child keeps the rank and the family seat, a serf's child is born on the same lord's manor, and the child of an enslaved parent belongs to the same household.
- **The Viking Age**: choose "The Viking Age" when you're born and live in Norway between AD 770 and 1030, paid in pieces of silver, with Norse patronymics (Bjorn's son is Bjornsson, his daughter Bjornsdottir, and your children take your name). Be born to a jarl, a chieftain, free farmers (karls) or as a thrall who can buy freedom, run for the forest or rise up. Go viking every summer to Lindisfarne, Ireland, Frankia, Seville or England for Danegeld, go berserk, buy a longship and captain your own crew until the skalds call you a sea-king; swear to a jarl's hird and stand in the shield wall at Hafrsfjord, Svolder or Stiklestad; compose a drápa that wins a gold ring as a skald; travel the halls as a völva seeing the future (beware Olaf Tryggvason); or work as a farmer, fisherman, smith, shipwright, weaver, fur hunter, trader or healer. Learn runes, wrestle, swim the fjord, play hnefatafl, watch horse fights, feast in the longhouse, speak at the Thing, make blóts to Odin, Thor, Freyr, Freyja and Njord, or be baptized as the White Christ arrives (by force under some kings). Settle feuds with wergild or holmgang, face outlawry at the Thing, sail west to settle Iceland or Greenland or reach Vinland, send traders to Hedeby or Miklagard, and die in battle to be carried to Valhalla. History happens: Lindisfarne, the sack of Paris, the Great Heathen Army, Harald Fairhair, the Danelaw, Normandy, the first Althing, Cnut's North Sea empire and Stamford Bridge.
- **Overthrow the throne**: in Rome, medieval England and the Viking Age you can plot to seize power. Patricians, senators, centurions and famous Romans can conspire against the emperor (recruit senators, bribe the Praetorian Guard, win over your legions); English nobles and knights can rebel against the king (win over the barons, hire mercenaries, call out your knights and archers). Every move builds strength but also suspicion, and if the plot is discovered or the strike fails you'll most likely be executed. Win and you become Emperor or Empress, King or Queen: keep the people and the Praetorians (or the barons) happy with games, donatives, feasts and monuments, purge rivals, raise taxes or go to war, and survive the assassins and rebels who will come for you. Your heir inherits the throne. People at the bottom can rise up too: enslaved Romans can plan a revolt like Spartacus, and English peasants can lead an uprising to free the serfs, though rebels who lose are crucified or hanged.
- **Become king**: tick "Born the emperor's firstborn" or "Born the king's firstborn" for Rome, medieval England or the Viking Age, and you grow up in the palace as heir to the throne (Caesar, Prince or Princess, the king's son or daughter) while your family rules and history goes its own way. An allowance from the treasury pays for learning statecraft at the council, winning over the Senate, the barons or the jarls, games and alms for the people, commanding legions or the king's army, and a royal marriage; all of it decides how strong your reign starts. When the ruler dies you are crowned (a child heir rules through a regent until 16, and a greedy regent may try to take the crown, as Richard of Gloucester did to the boy Edward V: win the great men to your side or keep your head down), or you can ask an aging parent to make you co-ruler, or take the throne by force. A wayward heir can be passed over for a younger brother or sister, and if you die as heir your own child inherits your place. Outsiders have roads too: a famous Roman consul, governor or centurion may be adopted by a childless emperor (as Nerva adopted Trajan) and inherit the empire; in the real boy-king years of England (1216, 1327, 1377, 1422) a great lord may be named Lord Protector and either guard the boy or take his crown; and in Norway a jarl or famous warrior can ask the Thing to make them king, as the farmers chose Haakon the Good. Growing up royal has its own events: the ruler falls gravely ill (sit at the bedside, quietly gather support, or pay for physicians), a famous teacher offers to educate you, envoys propose a royal match, a brother or sister starts courting the great men, and rumors that you're plotting reach the throne. In modern royal lives, the heir can ask an aging monarch to abdicate in their favor.
- **Ruling and conquest**: a ruler lives in the palace and sees the treasury, yearly income and how much danger the throne is in. Besides each era's own options (games and donatives, feasts for the barons, gold rings for the hird, monuments, purges, taxes), every ruler can hear petitions, set spies on the court, cut taxes, make peace, marry a foreign royal for an alliance, name an heir, and issue decrees from each era's own laws (make every free man a Roman citizen, abolish serfdom in England, free the thralls or order all Norway baptized). Reigns bring famines, envoys, uncovered plots and disasters, and history stops speaking of the old rulers. Every era has a real map with true coastlines: the Roman world, medieval Western Europe and the Viking North (with a Greenland inset) are split into provinces colored by who holds each one in the current year (Britannia becomes Roman in 43, England loses Normandy in 1204, Iceland is settled in 874), with bold borders between realms and a gold line around your own. Tap a province to see who holds it, how well it's defended, what it pays and what it borders, and invade it from there. Modern lives get a real world map of home, places lived and the wars of the day; tap a country for its life expectancy, taxes and money. Rulers can declare war on neighbors, invade border provinces by sending a general, leading the army in person or raising a huge army, settle empty lands, take provinces that pay tribute every year, and must defend against invasions, counterattacks and revolts in conquered land. Lose your capital and you lose the throne. When a reign ends, history gives the ruler an epithet earned by how they ruled (the Conqueror, the Builder, the Lawgiver, the Great, the Cruel, the Beloved, the Brief and more), shown with the reign's years, conquests and laws in the life recap and obituary. The family tree remembers every ancestor who wore the crown by title and epithet (Emperor Lucius the Builder, Queen Emma the Great, King Hugh), and counts the rulers in your family line.
- **Living through history**: start a life in any decade back to the 1900s (or a random year). Prices, wages and savings follow the era with inflation, life expectancy is lower in earlier decades, and things only exist once they were invented: no social media before 2004, smartphones and dating apps later still, no crypto before 2009, and stocks only trade once the company went public. History happens to you: drafts in World War I, World War II, Korea and Vietnam (report for duty or refuse), the home front, the Spanish flu, the 1929 crash and the Great Depression (layoffs and breadlines), the real stock market crashes and booms, the moon landing, the fall of the Berlin Wall, 9/11, the 2008 crisis and COVID-19, plus moments specific to your country. The life recap lists what you lived through.
- **Doctor visits & illnesses**: cancer, heart disease, high blood pressure and diabetes start silently, with only symptoms in your life story. Annual checkups and age-based screenings (blood panel, heart scan, cancer screening) catch them; ignore them and you end up in the ER. Cancer has stages 1–4 that worsen every untreated year, with surgery, chemotherapy (with side effects) and clinical trials, so early detection really matters. Untreated heart disease can cause a heart attack; bypass surgery or daily medication keeps it in check, and chronic illnesses can be managed with yearly prescriptions. Medical bills depend on where you live: public healthcare covers most of the cost in the UK, Canada, Europe, Japan and Korea, while in the US you're uninsured unless you buy a basic or premium plan, get one through a full-time job, or reach Medicare age. A health card shows your doctor, health, coverage and every condition's status. Medicine follows history: infections are far deadlier before penicillin (1942), diabetes before insulin (1922), there's no chemotherapy before 1950 and cancer survival improves decade by decade (immunotherapy from 2015), bypass surgery arrives in 1968, heart medication gets better with statins (1987), and therapy works better in later eras. Medicare starts in 1966, and before the Affordable Care Act (2014) insurers can turn you down for a pre-existing condition. COVID-19 hits in 2020–2022, with a vaccine choice in 2021. Family history matters: a parent, grandparent or sibling who died of cancer, a heart attack or a stroke raises your own risk, and your doctor screens you ten years earlier; smoking doubles your cancer and heart risk. Huge hospital bills can be negotiated, forgiven through financial assistance, or crowdfunded by friends, family and followers.
- **Running a business**: food trucks, coffee shops, gyms, restaurants, clothing brands, car dealerships, nightclubs and hotels each have their own revenue, costs, staff and margins. Set budget, standard or premium prices; hire and lay off staff; invest in quality; run marketing; and open (or close) locations. Customers leave star ratings that settle toward the quality you deliver for the price you charge, and a dashboard shows revenue, profit, value and a profit chart. Critics, health inspections, employee theft, rival openings, lawsuits, buyout offers and franchising pitches. Three losing years can close the doors. Tech startups work differently: grow users, burn cash, raise Seed through Series D rounds that dilute your stake, and either fold, sell to a big tech company or IPO. Once public, the company grows about as fast as the market, and slower the bigger it gets. As a founder you have four skills (management, marketing, finance and product) that grow as you run things or study, and shape every business you own: steadier quality, campaigns that land, leaner overhead, better products and higher sale prices. Hire a general manager to run a place while you work elsewhere, take a five-year business loan to expand, and launch something new (a safe crowd-pleaser or a bold swing). New situations: staff walkouts, supplier price hikes, influencer offers and "best in town" awards. Make the Forbes 30 Under 30 list, and become a mogul when your businesses are worth $50 million.
- **Health**: 29 illnesses, injuries and addictions. Doctors, herbalists, therapy, rehab and plastic surgery. Obituaries name the condition that actually killed you (heart disease, cancer…) instead of a passing cold. Old age follows a real-world curve, so taking care of yourself adds years (a well-treated life in the US lasts into the mid-80s) but nobody routinely reaches 95.
- **Activities**: gym, library, meditation, martial arts, instruments, social media, casino (blackjack, slots, roulette, horse racing), lottery, vacations, pets (8 species) and volunteering.
- **Crime & prison**: from shoplifting to jewelry heists, then trials with lawyers, prison gangs, appeals, escape attempts and good behavior.
- **Organized crime**: join a crime family and rise from Associate to Boss. You run jobs, earn respect, bribe detectives to cool the heat, survive FBI raids and rival families, or flip and become an informant.
- **Royalty**: choose to be born into a royal family. You have a place in the line of succession, royal duties, a popularity rating, tabloid scandals and arranged matches. You can be crowned, issue decrees, knight your friends, open Parliament, host state banquets, grant royal pardons, name which of your children inherits the crown, or abdicate. Royal life has its own events: state visits abroad, constitutional crises where the monarch must sign or refuse a hated law, tell-all memoirs by former aides, military service, charity patronages, suitors and tabloid stories. Long reigns are celebrated with Silver, Golden and Platinum Jubilees. A revolution may overthrow you. The crown passes down your family line.
- **Hobbies & skills**: coding, writing, singing, painting, cooking, comedy, chess and fitness. Skills boost related careers. You can write books that earn royalties, sell paintings, build apps, and enter competitions and open mics.
- **Wills**: name a main heir to inherit most of your estate.
- **Awards**: win Academy Awards, Grammys, MVPs, Pulitzers and even the Nobel Prize, with ceremonies where you choose how to show up.

## Install on Android

Every push builds the APKs in GitHub Actions. Open the **Actions** tab, pick the latest **Build Android APK** run, download the `TheLife-apk` artifact, and install `app-release.apk` on your phone. You'll need to allow installs from unknown sources.

## Build it yourself

Requirements: JDK 17+ and the Android SDK (API 35).

```bash
echo "sdk.dir=/path/to/Android/sdk" > local.properties
./gradlew assembleRelease        # → app/build/outputs/apk/release/app-release.apk
```

Or open the project in Android Studio and press Run.

The release build is signed with the debug key so it installs directly. Before publishing to Google Play, set up your own signing config in `app/build.gradle.kts`.

## Play in a browser

The game itself is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript in [`game/`](game/), with no build step. Open `game/index.html` in any browser, or host the folder as a PWA. Browsers block fonts on `file://` pages, so serve the folder (for example `python3 -m http.server` inside `game/`) to see the bundled font.

## Tests

```bash
node tests/simulate.js 200
```

This plays 200 families (plus their heirs) by tapping random menu items and choices every year. It fails on any exception or corrupted state, such as NaN money or stats out of range. Each family runs on its own seed, so a failure prints a command that replays it exactly, e.g. `node tests/simulate.js 1 123456`. Pass a second number to choose the starting seed.

```bash
node tests/events.js 120
```

The event sweep builds every event in the game and runs every choice across 120 random lives (modern, Rome, medieval England and the Viking Age, royal or not), both as-is and right after quitting the current job, so an event can never crash when the career or person it was about is gone.
